# Survey Design: Sleep and Academic Performance

## 1. Objectives of the survey

The overall aim of this survey is to investigate whether students' sleep duration and sleep quality are associated with their academic performance.

The specific objectives are to:

1. Identify how many hours students usually sleep on a weeknight.
2. Measure students' perceived sleep quality and how rested they feel during the day.
3. Examine whether insufficient or poor-quality sleep affects concentration, class attendance and study activities.
4. Measure students' self-reported academic performance.
5. Explore students' perceptions of the relationship between sleep and academic performance.
6. Identify common factors that prevent students from getting enough sleep.

These objectives support the broader research goal of understanding whether improving students' sleep habits could support learning and academic outcomes.

## 2. How the survey will be conducted and distributed

The survey will be administered as an anonymous online questionnaire using Google Forms. An online survey is suitable because it is convenient for students, inexpensive to administer and allows responses to be recorded automatically.

The survey link will be distributed to current students through the class learning-management system, the group’s approved communication channel and student email or social-media groups where permission has been obtained. The invitation will briefly explain the purpose of the research, the expected completion time and the voluntary nature of participation.

The survey will remain open for approximately one week. Participants will be asked to complete it only once. No names, student identification numbers or contact details will be collected.

## 3. Selected sample for the survey

The target population is current students at the institution. The proposed sample is approximately 50–100 students, depending on the number of valid responses received. A convenience sampling method will be used because the researchers will recruit students who are accessible through the class and approved student communication channels.

Participants should be current students aged 18 years or older. The sample should include students from different study levels and, where possible, different courses and study schedules. Participation will not depend on academic results, gender, nationality or employment status.

Convenience sampling may not represent every student in the institution, so the results will be reported as findings from the participants rather than as proof that the results apply to all students.

## 4. Structure of the survey

The survey contains seven close-ended questions and one open-ended question. The close-ended questions will provide quantitative data, while the final question will provide qualitative information.

### Survey questions

| No. | Question | Response type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | On a typical weeknight, how many hours do you sleep? | Less than 5 hours; 5 to less than 6 hours; 6 to less than 7 hours; 7–8 hours; More than 8 hours |
| 2 | How would you rate the overall quality of your sleep? | Very poor; Poor; Fair; Good; Very good |
| 3 | How often do you wake up feeling rested? | Never; Rarely; Sometimes; Often; Always |
| 4 | How often does tiredness make it difficult for you to concentrate on study or class activities? | Never; Rarely; Sometimes; Often; Always |
| 5 | During a typical month, how often do you miss or arrive late to class because you are tired or did not sleep enough? | Never; Once; 2–3 times; 4–5 times; More than 5 times |
| 6 | How would you rate your current academic performance? | Very poor; Poor; Average; Good; Very good |
| 7 | To what extent do you agree that getting enough sleep improves your academic performance? | Strongly disagree; Disagree; Neither agree nor disagree; Agree; Strongly agree |
| 8 | In your opinion, what are the main factors that affect your sleep and academic performance? | Open-text response |

Questions 1–7 are designed to produce structured data that can be compared across participants. Question 8 allows participants to describe experiences or factors that may not be included in the fixed response options.

### Proposed analysis

The quantitative responses will be analysed using frequencies, percentages, averages and median values where appropriate. Results will be displayed in tables and charts. Cross-tabulations will be used to compare sleep-duration or sleep-quality groups with concentration difficulties and self-rated academic performance. A Spearman correlation may be used to examine the association between ordered sleep-quality responses and ordered academic-performance responses. The analysis will describe association only and will not claim that sleep directly causes academic performance.

The open-ended responses will be analysed using thematic analysis. The researchers will read all responses, identify repeated ideas, create codes, group similar codes into themes and report the main themes with a few short anonymised examples. Responses will not be reported in a way that could identify an individual participant.

## 5. Ethical considerations

The survey will follow these ethical principles:

- **Informed consent:** The first page will explain the purpose of the study, what participation involves, the approximate completion time, how the data will be used and the contact details of the research group or supervisor. Participants will continue only after confirming that they understand the information and agree to participate.
- **Voluntary participation:** Participation will be optional. Students will not be pressured by teachers, group members or classmates to respond.
- **Right to withdraw:** Participants may stop completing the survey at any time before submitting it. Because the survey is anonymous, it may not be possible to remove an individual response after submission if it cannot be identified.
- **Privacy and anonymity:** The survey will not request names, student numbers, email addresses, precise locations or other unnecessary identifying information. The link will not be configured to collect email addresses.
- **Confidentiality:** Only the research group and authorised supervisor will access the response file. Results will be reported in aggregate form rather than as individual responses.
- **Minimising harm:** Questions will be limited to sleep and study experiences. Participants will not be required to disclose medical diagnoses, family information or exact grades. A participant may skip any question that is not compulsory and may stop if a question makes them uncomfortable.
- **Secure data handling:** The downloaded response file will be stored in a password-protected location, shared only with authorised group members and deleted or archived according to the course requirements after the project is completed.
- **Fair recruitment:** The invitation will use neutral language and will not promise rewards or imply that participation will affect grades. Students will not be excluded unfairly on the basis of gender, culture, nationality or academic ability.
- **Responsible reporting:** The researchers will not change, fabricate or selectively remove responses to support a preferred conclusion. Limitations such as convenience sampling, self-reported answers and possible response bias will be acknowledged.
