# Literature Review: Artificial Intelligence in Higher Education

## 1. Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become an important technology in higher education. AI technologies, including generative AI tools, chatbots, intelligent tutoring systems, and large language models, are increasingly being used to support teaching, learning, assessment, programming, research, and academic activities.

The development of generative AI tools such as ChatGPT has increased interest in how AI can improve educational experiences. AI can provide students with personalised feedback, explanations, writing assistance, programming support, and opportunities for independent learning. However, the increasing use of AI also creates concerns related to academic integrity, student dependence, inaccurate information, privacy, assessment validity, and the development of critical-thinking skills.

Recent systematic reviews indicate that AI can provide positive educational outcomes, but the results are not uniformly positive. For example, research has identified benefits such as improved academic performance, personalised learning, motivation, and cognitive support, while also identifying risks including inaccurate information, technological dependence, privacy concerns, and insufficient AI literacy.

Therefore, this literature review examines existing research on the use of AI in higher education and identifies common findings, research gaps, methodologies, benefits, and challenges.

## 2. Research Question

**How does the use of Artificial Intelligence affect teaching, learning, and academic outcomes in higher education?**

## 5. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

### Inclusion criteria

Articles will be included when they:

1. Are published in peer-reviewed academic journals or conferences.
2. Directly discuss Artificial Intelligence or Generative AI.
3. Focus on education or higher education.
4. Discuss teaching, learning, student outcomes, AI adoption, or academic integrity.
5. Are written in English.
6. Provide sufficient information about their research methodology and findings.
7. Preferably have been published between 2020 and 2026 to reflect recent AI developments.

### Exclusion criteria

Articles will be excluded when they:

1. Are not related to education.
2. Are opinion articles without research evidence.
3. Are duplicated records.
4. Are not available from a reliable academic source.
5. Focus only on technical AI development without an educational application.
6. Do not provide sufficient information relevant to the research question.

# 6. Literature Review Process

The literature search will follow a structured process.

First, keywords related to Artificial Intelligence and higher education will be identified. These keywords will be combined using Boolean operators such as AND and OR.

Second, the selected keywords will be searched through academic databases including IEEE Xplore, Scopus, Google Scholar, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, and ACM Digital Library.

Third, the titles and abstracts of potentially relevant papers will be reviewed. Articles that are unrelated to the research question will be removed.

Fourth, the remaining papers will be assessed according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Finally, 10 valuable articles will be selected for detailed analysis. Information about their research methods, findings, themes, limitations, and research gaps will be compared and synthesised.

# 7. Analysis of Selected Literature

## Paper 1: Ogunleye et al. – A Systematic Review of Generative AI for Teaching and Learning Practice

Ogunleye et al. conducted a systematic review examining the use of Generative AI in teaching and learning. The review used the PRISMA approach and examined a large collection of research indexed through Scopus.

The study found that Generative AI has considerable potential for supporting teaching, learning, assessment, and educational delivery. However, the authors identified a need for clearer guidelines and frameworks for effective use of Generative AI in higher education.

**Methodology:** Systematic literature review using PRISMA.

**Key finding:** Generative AI has significant potential in education, but institutions require appropriate policies, guidelines, and frameworks.

**Research gap:** More interdisciplinary and multidimensional research is required to understand how Generative AI should be integrated into higher education.

**Contribution:** This paper provides an overall understanding of the development of Generative AI research in education and helps establish the background for this literature review.

## Paper 2: An, Koh and Liu – Generative Artificial Intelligence in Higher Education

An, Koh and Liu conducted a systematic review of 39 peer-reviewed empirical studies concerning student use of Generative AI in higher education.

The review identified several ways students use Generative AI for learning tasks. The findings indicate that actual learning outcomes were predominantly successful, although students' perceived outcomes were more mixed.

**Methodology:** Systematic literature review of empirical studies.

**Key finding:** Generative AI can support student learning, but the benefits and perceived outcomes vary between students and contexts.

**Research gap:** More empirical research is required to understand the conditions under which AI produces positive learning outcomes.

**Contribution:** This paper directly supports the research question because it provides evidence concerning student use of Generative AI and learning outcomes.

## Paper 3: Zhang, Lai and Yu – Generative Artificial Intelligence in K-12 Education

Zhang, Lai and Yu reviewed 45 studies published between 2020 and 2024 concerning Generative AI in K-12 education.

The review identified benefits including improved academic performance, cognitive abilities, motivation, and personalised learning. However, challenges included inaccurate AI-generated information, technological dependence, privacy concerns, and limited AI literacy.

**Methodology:** Systematic literature review and thematic analysis.

**Key finding:** AI can support learning and personalisation but introduces risks associated with accuracy, dependence, privacy, and AI literacy.

**Research gap:** Much existing research examines short-term effects, while longitudinal evidence remains limited.

**Contribution:** Although the paper focuses on K-12 education, its findings identify important themes that can also be investigated in higher education.

## Paper 4: Liang et al. – Generative AI in Education from a Human-AI Interaction Perspective

Liang et al. conducted a systematic review of Generative AI in education with particular attention to human-AI interaction.

The study highlights that the effectiveness of AI-powered educational applications depends not only on the technology itself but also on how students and educators interact with AI.

**Methodology:** Systematic literature review.

**Key finding:** Human-AI interaction is an important factor influencing the effectiveness of Generative AI in education.

**Research gap:** More research is needed to understand how interaction between humans and AI affects educational outcomes.

**Contribution:** This paper strengthens the literature review by demonstrating that AI effectiveness should be evaluated together with human behaviour and interaction.

## Paper 5: Marzano – Generative Artificial Intelligence in Teaching and Learning

Marzano conducted a systematic review of Generative AI in K-12 teaching and learning.

The research provides evidence of increasing use of Generative AI in education and discusses the potential benefits and challenges associated with its implementation.

**Methodology:** Systematic review.

**Key finding:** Generative AI can provide educational opportunities, but effective implementation requires appropriate educational strategies.

**Research gap:** Additional empirical studies are needed to understand how AI can be integrated into educational practice effectively.

**Contribution:** The paper provides evidence about implementation challenges and educational applications of Generative AI.

## Paper 6: Gunsaldi et al. – Generative AI and Student Learning Outcomes

Gunsaldi et al. conducted a systematic review examining the impact of Generative AI applications on student learning outcomes in science education.

The review included 12 studies and found positive contributions to academic achievement, conceptual understanding, scientific literacy, digital literacy, personalised learning, motivation, and emotional outcomes.

**Methodology:** Systematic literature review using PRISMA.

**Key finding:** Generative AI can positively affect several aspects of student learning.

**Research gap:** Further studies are required to determine whether these positive effects remain over longer periods.

**Contribution:** The study provides evidence that AI may improve measurable learning outcomes and supports further investigation of AI's educational impact.

## Paper 7: Yalwa et al. – Generative AI in Higher Education with Emphasis on Programming and Computer Science

Yalwa et al. examined Generative AI in higher education, particularly programming and computer science education.

The study highlights applications such as code generation, debugging, conceptual explanation, and personalised learning support.

**Methodology:** Systematic literature review.

**Key finding:** Generative AI is increasingly being used to support programming and computer science learning.

**Research gap:** Existing research remains fragmented across educational outcomes, adoption, perceptions, and long-term use.

**Contribution:** This paper is particularly relevant to computing students because it demonstrates how AI can support programming and technical learning.

## Paper 8: Ogunleye et al. – AI Integration, Curriculum and Assessment

The literature reviewed by Ogunleye et al. also highlights the importance of integrating Generative AI into curriculum, assessment, and teaching practices rather than treating AI only as a tool for generating answers.

The research indicates that universities need to consider how AI changes traditional assessment and learning practices.

**Methodology:** Systematic review and bibliometric analysis.

**Key finding:** AI requires changes in curriculum, assessment design, teaching practice, and institutional policy.

**Research gap:** More research is required to develop practical frameworks for responsible AI integration.

**Contribution:** This work connects AI technology with institutional and educational practice.

## Paper 9: Neumann et al. – AI Adoption in Higher Education

Neumann et al. investigated Generative AI adoption among higher education students and examined institutional policies.

The study found widespread use of Generative AI, particularly ChatGPT, for research assistance, programming support, and text processing. It also identified uncertainty among students regarding institutional AI policies.

**Methodology:** Case study combining document analysis and an online survey.

**Key finding:** Students are adopting AI quickly, while institutional policies and student understanding of those policies may not always keep pace.

**Research gap:** Universities need clearer and more operational approaches to AI governance, assessment, and academic integrity.

**Contribution:** This paper demonstrates that AI's educational impact is influenced not only by the technology but also by institutional policy and governance.

## Paper 10: Elnaffar, Rashidi and Abualkishik – AI in Programming Education

Elnaffar, Rashidi and Abualkishik reviewed peer-reviewed studies on AI agents in programming education.

The review identified benefits including programming support, personalised feedback, improved learning outcomes, and increased efficiency. However, it also highlighted problems such as over-reliance, superficial learning, AI errors, and academic-integrity concerns.

**Methodology:** Systematic review of peer-reviewed studies.

**Key finding:** AI can provide strong programming support but may create dependency and reduce deeper learning when students rely on it excessively.

**Research gap:** More research is required on effective instructional frameworks, human oversight, and responsible AI use.

**Contribution:** This paper provides specific evidence about the advantages and risks of AI for computer science and programming education.

# 8. Common Themes Across the Literature

Several common themes appear across the selected literature.

### 8.1 Personalised Learning

Many studies identify personalised learning as an important advantage of AI. AI tools can provide students with explanations, feedback, and learning support based on individual needs.

### 8.2 Improved Learning Outcomes

Several studies report positive effects on academic performance, conceptual understanding, motivation, and learning support. However, the size and consistency of these benefits vary between studies.

### 8.3 Student Dependence

A major concern is excessive dependence on AI. Students may rely on AI to produce answers instead of developing their own reasoning, writing, problem-solving, and critical-thinking skills.

### 8.4 Academic Integrity

Generative AI creates challenges for traditional assessment because students can use AI to produce written responses, solve problems, or generate computer code.

### 8.5 AI Literacy

Students and teachers need sufficient AI literacy to understand the capabilities and limitations of AI. Users need to recognise that AI-generated information may be inaccurate.

### 8.6 Privacy and Ethical Issues

The literature identifies concerns about privacy, data use, transparency, bias, and responsible use of AI.

### 8.7 Institutional Policies

Universities need clear policies explaining acceptable and unacceptable AI use. Unclear policies can create confusion among students and teachers.

# 9. Key Research Gaps

The literature identifies several important research gaps.

First, many studies focus on the short-term effects of AI. More longitudinal studies are needed to determine the long-term effects on student learning and academic development.

Second, research findings are sometimes based on students' perceptions rather than objective measurements of learning outcomes. More empirical studies using controlled or comparative approaches are required.

Third, there is limited agreement about how universities should regulate AI use while still allowing students to benefit from the technology.

Fourth, more research is needed into the effects of AI on critical thinking, creativity, problem-solving, and independent learning.

Fifth, AI technology is developing rapidly, meaning that research can become outdated quickly. Continuous research is therefore required.

Finally, more research should examine differences between disciplines, student groups, educational institutions, and cultural contexts.

# 12. Conclusion

The literature indicates that Artificial Intelligence has significant potential to transform higher education. AI can support personalised learning, provide feedback, assist programming and research, and improve some aspects of student learning.

However, the literature also demonstrates that AI is not automatically beneficial. Excessive dependence, inaccurate information, academic-integrity concerns, privacy issues, and insufficient AI literacy can reduce the benefits of the technology.

A major finding across the literature is that successful AI implementation requires a balance between technological assistance and human learning. Universities therefore need appropriate policies, AI literacy education, responsible assessment strategies, and continued research.

The major research gaps include the lack of long-term evidence, inconsistent measurement of learning outcomes, limited research into critical thinking and independent learning, and uncertainty about institutional AI policies.

Overall, the literature supports the view that AI can positively influence higher education when it is integrated responsibly and used to support rather than replace human learning and teaching.
